Feed restored — and backfilled

The comment ingestion that went dark on 22 June is live again, and the six-week gap came back with it. July went from zero scraped comments to 2,251; August is already at 223. 2,854 new top-level comments are now readable, taking the working corpus from 13,681 to 16,530. Everything below has been re-cut on the full set, and the six weeks we thought we had lost turn out to contain the clearest trend line in the account.

Summary

Six things worth your attention

Good news
Trust complaints have collapsed. Scam accusations fell from 2.2% of comments to 0.7% in the six-week window, and subscription or service complaints fell from 6.9% to 4.8%. Reply coverage went from 45% to 65%. Whatever changed on the service and checkout side is working, and it is visible in public where prospects read it.
New #1 objection
Sweeteners are now the largest and fastest-growing complaint, up from 7.4% to 8.7% of comments while everything else around it improved. It has overtaken price. We are also still running a live page that claims "No Artificial Sweeteners" on a sucralose-and-erythritol product, which is what turns an ingredient question into a credibility problem.
They wrote our ad
Customers are now policing the dose for us. The second most-liked comment of the window is a customer telling other buyers "keep it to one serving or less before you know how your body reacts, and one serving is 17 pieces." That is the half-a-bag angle, in a real customer's voice, already validated by likes. It should be briefed verbatim.
Free demand
Black licorice keeps growing and is still unanswered — now 571 mentions and 1,408 likes, up from 459 and 1,008 six weeks ago. The ad that generates it burned $32.7K at 0.92 ROAS for a $14.2K loss. We are paying to collect a product request we do nothing with.
Discontinued hit
The single most-liked comment of the entire window asks for a flavor we stopped making. "Will the ones with the chili ever come back. I used to have them on automatic order." 78 likes. Chili and cinnamon together pull 107 mentions and 396 likes. A discontinued SKU with proven repeat-purchase behavior is a cheaper win than a new one.
Where the money went
One creative absorbed $156K and lost $77K in the six-week window, and it is also the most-commented post in the account. Comment volume is not a quality signal. It measures attention, and attention on a losing creative is an expensive way to collect feedback.
The corpus

Brand sentiment

Every Meta ad we run collects a public comment thread. Cold prospects read it before they buy, which makes it a de-facto second landing page that we do not write. It is also the only unfiltered, unincentivised, statistically meaningful voice-of-customer source we own.

Trust

The service fix is showing up in public

Scam accusations fell 68% and subscription complaints fell 30% across the six weeks, while reply coverage climbed from 45% to 65%. This is the clearest before-and-after in the whole corpus and it happened without a creative change. Protect it: the comment section is the only part of the funnel a prospect reads that we do not control.

Risk

Sweeteners overtook price

Up to 8.7% while every neighbouring objection improved, making it the account's largest complaint. Erythritol 488 mentions, sucralose 348. The glp-2 page still says "No Artificial Sweeteners" and disparages sugar alcohols on a product containing both. That contradiction is the manufacturing line for the scam comments we just spent six weeks reducing.
