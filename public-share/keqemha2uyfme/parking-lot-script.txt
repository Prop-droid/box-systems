## CH1 — THE CATCH (0:00 to 1:00)

### Cut A opening (hook 1)

SEE: Gym parking lot, late afternoon. Her car. She's in gym clothes, kit bag untouched on the back seat, eating out of a candy bag held low, below the window line.
VO: I was forty nine years old and I had learned to eat below the window line.

SEE: Knuckles on the glass. She jumps. Daniel, gym bag on his shoulder, looking in at her. A wrapper stuck to her lip.
VO: nothing.

SEE: He doesn't shout. He looks at her for three long seconds, turns, walks into the gym alone. She watches through the windshield until the door swings shut.
VO: He didn't shout. That was the worst part.

SEE: She sits. Peels the wrapper off her lip. Looks at it.
VO: Six weeks later he moved in with a twenty nine year old.

SEE: Front door. His suitcase. His hand on the handle. Her in the doorway.
VO: He told me it was about discipline.

SEE: Her face, close, holding it together and failing.
VO: And the only reason I didn't crumble is my sister got on a plane.

### Cut B opening (hook 4) — replaces the first 20 seconds, then joins at "Knuckles on the glass"

SEE: Doctor's office. A printout slides across the desk. REDUCE SUGARY SNACKS. Her hand takes it.
VO: My doctor gave me a piece of paper that said reduce sugary snacks.

SEE: Her in the car in the medical center parking lot, engine off, printout on the passenger seat, eating.
VO: In eleven years nobody has ever told me what to do with the part of me that wants it.

SEE: Cut to gym parking lot, same posture, different day. Kit bag untouched on the back seat.
VO: I was forty nine and I had learned to eat below the window line.

## CH2 — THE COLLAPSE (1:00 to 2:10)

SEE: Half an empty closet. Bare hangers swinging. One shirt of his left behind on the rail.
VO: Twenty two years.

SEE: She sits on the end of the bed holding the shirt. She does not cry.
VO: She's twenty nine. I'm forty nine. I did that math every night for a month.

SEE: Phone in her hand, thumb scrolling a grid of the woman's photos. Gym, gym, gym.
VO: He didn't leave because she was younger.

SEE: Close on a photo. Then on her face.
VO: He left because she was, and I'm quoting him, someone who finishes what she starts.

SEE: Phone face down on the duvet. She opens the nightstand drawer. Candy bag.
VO: I ate two bags of the gas station stuff that night.

SEE: The drawer, wider. It's full of wrappers. More than should fit.
VO: I want to be honest about that part. It got worse before anything got better.

## CH3 — THREE YEARS OF IT (2:10 to 3:40)

SEE: Hard cut back. Same kitchen, three years earlier, brighter grade. She's fine. One bag, one evening, no big deal.
VO: It started as a treat. It always does.

SEE: Hiding montage, hard cuts, no VO. Glovebox. Coat pocket. Behind the paper towels. A zip pouch inside the gym bag inside the trunk.
VO: nothing.

SEE: Gym entrance from across the lot. She's in the car. She does not go in.
VO: My gym membership was forty dollars a month for two years. I went eleven times.

SEE: Gas station forecourt. Same pump, same slot, four quick repeats with the light changing across them. The guy at the register nods and stops asking.
VO: I ate in the parking lot because a wrapper in my own trash was evidence.

SEE: Bathroom scale. Her feet on it. She doesn't look down.
VO: Thirty four pounds in three years. I could have told you exactly where every single one of them came from.

SEE: The printout going into a kitchen drawer, under other paper.
VO: My doctor gave me a piece of paper that said reduce sugary snacks.

SEE: Her in the car in the medical center lot, eating, printout on the passenger seat.
VO: In eleven years nobody has ever told me what to do with the part of me that wants it.

## CH4 — THE ALLY (3:40 to 5:00)

SEE: Airport arrivals at night. RITA comes through the doors. Sixty one, upright, bright, moving fast.
VO: My sister Rita is twelve years older than me.

SEE: Kitchen, late. Rita drops her coat, opens her carry-on, and the first thing out of it is a candy bag. She puts it on the counter.
VO: She got in at eleven at night and the first thing out of her bag was candy.

SEE: Rita eating them, talking with her mouth full, completely unbothered. Her watching, not understanding.
VO: I sat there watching my sixty one year old sister eat candy straight off a plane and I felt something I did not expect to feel, which was jealous.

SEE: Rita catches the look. Slides the bag across the counter.
VO: She said, sis. You don't have a discipline problem. You have a bag problem.

SEE: Close on the bag. Then on her face, not buying it.
VO: I said there's no way.

SEE: Rita, deadpan, not selling: "Just watch."
VO: nothing.

SEE: Her taking one. Bracing. Chewing. Stopping mid-chew. Looking at the bag.
VO: I'd eaten the healthy ones before. They taste like a scented candle. I had my mouth ready to spit it in the sink.

SEE: She takes four more.
VO: I did not spit it in the sink.

## CH5 — THE NUMBERS (5:00 to 6:00)

SEE: The two bags side by side on the counter under the kitchen light. Hers from the gas station. Rita's.
VO: So Rita turned both bags over.

SEE: Macro on the two panels, slow push.
VO: The one I'd been buying every day for three years. And this one. Twenty six grams of fiber. Three grams of sugar. Seventy calories.

SEE: Her holding both, looking between them.
VO: I read it three times because I assumed I had a decimal point wrong.

SEE: Rita shrugging, pouring more into her hand, already bored of the conversation.
VO: Rita said the fiber's the bonus. She said the point is I get to keep the ten minutes in the car.

SEE: Her, quiet, holding the bag, taking that in.
VO: Nobody had ever offered me the ten minutes. Every plan I'd ever been handed started by taking them away.

## CH6 — THE WEEKS (6:00 to 7:30)

VO: Week two, I still stopped at the same gas station. Same time, same spot. I just brought my own.

SEE: The nightstand drawer opens. Candy bag inside. Same drawer as CH2.
VO: Same drawer. Same time of night. Same ten minutes.

SEE: Close on the bag in her hand.
VO: I never gave it up. I swapped it.

## CH9 — CLOSE (9:10 to 9:30)

SEE: Her car, evening, parked outside her own house. Window down. Interior light on. Bag on the passenger seat.

VO: I just don't park where nobody can see me anymore.

SEE: End card. Pack. Guarantee badge. No spoken offer.
