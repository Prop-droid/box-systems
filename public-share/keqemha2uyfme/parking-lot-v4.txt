Locked hooks: 1 and 4 (two cuts, one variation each).
Format: Pixar-style 3D, 9:16, 2-4s shots, static camera, burned-in line-level captions on every spoken line.
Rule: VO says only what the picture can't. Nothing showable is narrated.

Cast: HER (49). DANIEL, husband (51). RITA, her sister (61). THE 29 YEAR OLD. THE CLERK. KATE.

## What v4 fixed
1. Daniel speaks. His CH1 line is the film's spine and she hands it back to him in CH8.
2. The wrapper room. Three years of bags as one surreal frame.
3. Rita is the comedy. Blunt, eating in every shot, zero sympathy, eight lines.
4. The hiding montage escalates to the decoy almonds.
5. The gym runs four times and pays off in the last shot.
6. The 29 year old gets the gasp line.

## CH1 — THE CATCH (0:00 to 1:10)

### Cut A opening (hook 1)

SEE: Gym parking lot, late afternoon. Her car. Gym clothes on, kit bag untouched on the back seat. She's eating out of a candy bag held low, below the window line.
VO: I was forty nine years old and I had learned to eat below the window line.

SEE: Knuckles on the glass. She jumps. DANIEL, gym bag on his shoulder, bending down to the window. A wrapper stuck to her lip.
VO: nothing.

SEE: He does not shout. He crouches to her level. His voice is kind, which is worse.
DANIEL: I'm not mad.

SEE: Her, frozen, wrapper still on her lip.
DANIEL: I just wish you'd finish something.

SEE: He straightens up, taps the roof twice, and walks into the gym alone. She watches through the windshield until the door swings shut.
VO: He tapped the roof of the car. Like a taxi.

SEE: She sits there. Peels the wrapper off her lip. Looks at it.
VO: Six weeks later he moved in with a twenty nine year old.

SEE: Front door. His suitcase. His hand on the handle. Her in the doorway.
VO: He told me it was about discipline.

SEE: Her face, close, holding it together and failing.
VO: And the only reason I didn't crumble is my sister got on a plane.

### Cut B opening (hook 4) — replaces the first 20 seconds, then joins at "Knuckles on the glass"

SEE: Doctor's office. A printout slides across the desk. REDUCE SUGARY SNACKS. Her hand takes it.
VO: My doctor gave me a piece of paper that said reduce sugary snacks.

SEE: Her in the car in the medical center lot, engine off, printout on the passenger seat, eating.
VO: In eleven years nobody has ever told me what to do with the part of me that wants it.

SEE: Cut to gym parking lot, same posture, different day. Kit bag untouched on the back seat.
VO: I was forty nine and I had learned to eat below the window line.

## CH2 — THE COLLAPSE (1:10 to 2:10)

SEE: Half an empty closet. Bare hangers still swinging. One shirt of his left on the rail.
VO: Twenty two years.

SEE: She sits on the end of the bed holding the shirt. She does not cry.
VO: She's twenty nine. I'm forty nine. I did that math every night for a month.

SEE: Phone in her hand, thumb scrolling a grid of the woman's photos. Gym. Gym. Gym.
VO: He didn't leave because she was younger.

SEE: Close on one photo. Then on her face.
VO: He left because she was, and I'm quoting, someone who finishes what she starts.

SEE: Phone face down. She opens the nightstand drawer. Candy bag.
VO: I ate two bags of the gas station stuff that night.

SEE: The drawer, wider. Wrappers packed in, more than should fit.
VO: I want to be honest about that part. It got worse before anything got better.

## CH3 — THREE YEARS OF IT (2:10 to 3:20)

SEE: Hard cut back. Same kitchen, three years earlier, brighter grade. One bag, one evening, no big deal.
VO: It started as a treat. It always does.

SEE: Hiding montage. Hard cuts, no VO, each one escalating. Coat pocket. Behind the paper towels. Duct-taped under the driver's seat. A zip pouch inside the gym bag inside the trunk.
VO: nothing.

SEE: Glovebox opens. A bag of almonds sits at the front, immaculate, arranged. Her hand moves it forward so it's the first thing anyone would see, then closes the glovebox on the candy behind it.
VO: I bought almonds I never ate and put them where people would look.

SEE: Gas station forecourt. Same pump, same slot, four quick repeats with the light changing across them. THE CLERK starts reaching for her order before she's through the door.
VO: The guy at the register stopped asking after about eight months.

SEE: Gym parking lot from across the street. Her car. She does not go in.
VO: My gym membership was forty dollars a month for two years. I went eleven times.

## CH4 — THE ROOM (3:20 to 3:50)

SEE: She opens the door to a spare room. Wrappers. Floor to window sill, wall to wall, three years of them, spilling toward her feet. She stands in the doorway. Not a dream sequence, played completely straight.
VO: One thousand and ninety five bags.

SEE: Slow push in. She doesn't move.
VO: I did the math once.

SEE: The door closes on it.
VO: I only did it once.

SEE: Bathroom scale. Her feet on it. She does not look down.
VO: Thirty four pounds. I could have told you exactly where every single one of them came from.

SEE: The doctor's printout going into a kitchen drawer, under other paper.
VO: My doctor gave me a piece of paper that said reduce sugary snacks.

## CH5 — RITA (3:50 to 5:20)

SEE: Airport arrivals, night. RITA comes through the doors. Sixty one, upright, fast, already eating something.
VO: My sister Rita is twelve years older than me.

SEE: Rita hugs her, pulls back, looks at her properly.
RITA: You look tired. Not old. Tired. Different thing.

SEE: Kitchen, late. Rita drops her coat and the first thing out of her carry-on is a candy bag. Onto the counter.
VO: She got in at eleven at night and the first thing out of her bag was candy.

SEE: Her staring at it. Rita already eating, not looking up.
RITA: Don't look at me like that.

SEE: Her opening the kitchen drawer to find a coaster. The doctor's printout is right on top. Rita reads it upside down.
RITA: Reduce sugary snacks. Genius. Did he tell you to breathe as well?

SEE: Rita opens the glovebox on her way past the hall table. Sees the almonds. Holds them up.
RITA: What the f*** are the almonds for?

SEE: Her, caught. Nothing to say.
VO: nothing.

SEE: Rita puts the almonds down and slides her own bag across the counter.
RITA: Sis. You don't have a discipline problem. You have a bag problem.

SEE: Close on the bag. Then on her face, not buying it.
VO: I said there's no way.

SEE: Rita shrugs, keeps eating, does not argue.
RITA: Okay.

SEE: Her taking one. Bracing for it. Chewing. Stopping mid-chew. Looking at the bag in her hand.
VO: I'd eaten the healthy ones before. They taste like a scented candle. I had my mouth ready to spit it in the sink.

SEE: Rita, not looking up from her phone.
RITA: Mm hm.

SEE: She takes four more.
VO: I did not spit it in the sink.

## CH6 — THE NUMBERS (5:20 to 6:10)

SEE: The two bags side by side under the kitchen light. Hers from the gas station. Rita's.
VO: So Rita turned both bags over.

VO: The one I'd been buying every day for three years. And this one. Twenty six grams of fiber. Three grams of sugar. Seventy calories.

VO: I read it three times because I assumed I had a decimal point wrong.

SEE: Rita pouring more into her hand, already bored of the conversation.
RITA: The fiber's a bonus. I don't care about the fiber. I care that you get to sit in your car.

SEE: Her, quiet, holding the bag.
VO: Nobody had ever offered me the ten minutes. Every plan I'd ever been handed started by taking them away.

SEE: Front door, morning. Rita leaving, suitcase up. She reaches for Rita's bag on the counter. Rita gets there first and puts it in her own coat pocket.

## CH7 — THE WEEKS (6:10 to 7:40)

SEE: Gas station forecourt. Same pump, same slot, same time of day. THE CLERK reaches for her order. She waves him off and holds up her own bag. He looks genuinely wounded.
VO: Week two, I still stopped at the same gas station. Same time, same spot. I just brought my own.

SEE: Gym parking lot. Her car. She's eating. Different bag. Still not going in.
VO: I want to be clear that I still didn't go to the gym.

SEE: Kitchen, week four, daylight. The bag out on the counter. Nobody home to hide from and she leaves it out anyway.
VO: Week four I stopped putting it away. Not because I quit. Because there was nothing left to hide.

SEE: Glovebox opens. She takes out the almonds and drops them in a trash can. Small, satisfying.
VO: nothing.

SEE: Closet, week seven. She pulls the green dress from the back, where it has been.
VO: nothing.

SEE: Her in it. The zipper goes all the way up.
VO: Week seven.

SEE: Bathroom, the scale. She steps on. This time she looks down.
VO: Week nine I looked at the number instead of stepping straight off.

SEE: The mirror. She stands there a second longer than she needs to.
VO: I'm not going to tell you a new woman was standing in the mirror. It was me. It was the version of me that wasn't lying to anybody.

SEE: The nightstand drawer opens. Candy bag inside. Same drawer as CH2.
VO: Same drawer. Same time of night. Same ten minutes.

SEE: Close on the bag in her hand.
VO: I never gave it up. I swapped it.

## CH8 — THE RESTAURANT (7:40 to 8:50)

SEE: Restaurant, evening. Her at a table by the window. Green dress. She looks up as the door opens.
VO: I wasn't there for him. I want that on the record.

SEE: DANIEL comes in with THE 29 YEAR OLD. Coat off, chair pulled out. He hasn't seen her.
VO: nothing.

SEE: He scans the room the way you do. Stops. Comes back. Finds her.
VO: nothing. Hold it. Let his face do the work.

SEE: His mouth opens slightly. The 29 year old follows his eyes and finds her too. She looks at her a long moment. Then at him.
THE 29 YEAR OLD: You said she let herself go.

SEE: Daniel, no answer. The 29 year old sits back down without taking her eyes off him.
VO: nothing.

SEE: Her hand moves to the table. An open bag of candy next to her wine. She takes one. Holds Daniel's eye. Eats it.
VO: He left me over a bag of candy in a parking lot.

SEE: She holds the bag up an inch. Just an inch. Speaks for the only time in the film.
HER: I finished it.

SEE: She turns back to her conversation. He is still standing.
VO: nothing.
