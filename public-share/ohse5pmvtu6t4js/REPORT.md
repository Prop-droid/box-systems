# Shameless creative report: what is winning by funnel, what customers are telling us, and where it cashes on the way to $115M

Meta, 2026-08-10 to 2026-09-06, 174 creatives with $400+ spend, $385,295. Source: creative_dashboard (SHA, facebook), ClickUp briefs, winners archive, comment digests Sep 1 and Sep 8. Built 2026-09-08. HTML twin: index.html (published on the Funnel).

Four weeks of Meta spend, every creative over $400, read against the winner canon, the landing-page funnel and the last two weeks of ad comments.
The short version: **the account sells candy abundance, not fiber**. What wins is a box full of flavors, a number the viewer can check against candy they already eat, and proof it sells out at Target.
What loses is curiosity copy that the click resolves, problem-first health framing, and high-hook talking heads.

Mechanism: Show the box
### The haul: show the box, not the bag
SH-16975-2SH-16180-1SH-17155-4SH-15611-1
The four biggest videos in the account are all a shipper box of 12 bags being opened, spilled, or pulled from. [SH-16975-2](https://asset-proxy-326409019927.us-central1.run.app/?channel=meta&id=884581671385763&type=video) opens on a burned-in caption, _HOW IS THIS EVEN LEGAL?_, over the box: $21k at 1.19 ROAS and 45.5% LP to ATC. [SH-16180-1](https://asset-proxy-326409019927.us-central1.run.app/?channel=meta&id=2064374784493597&type=video) counts the bags out loud ("not one, not two... 12 bags"): $29k, 39% ATC. [SH-17155-4](https://asset-proxy-326409019927.us-central1.run.app/?channel=meta&id=2196653511192793&type=video) does it with hands only, no face, 1.41 ROAS. These have the lowest hook rates in the top 20 (13 to 18%) and the best conversion, which is the account's hook-rate law showing again: the people who stop for a box are buyers.

**The hook is a mechanic, not a line.** The caption cold open (no VO for four seconds, box-open sound carrying the audio) beat every spoken opener in the family. Quantity on screen is value felt before price is said.

Mechanism: Pre-living the order
### Pack it for Sarah: comment-reply order packing
SH-12937-7SH-15191-5SH-16212-10
Three warehouse videos hold the three highest LP-to-ATC rates in the account: [SH-15191-5](https://asset-proxy-326409019927.us-central1.run.app/?channel=meta&id=907098952427429&type=video) 49.9%, [SH-12937-7](https://asset-proxy-326409019927.us-central1.run.app/?channel=meta&id=1217655417180596&type=video) 48.2%, [SH-16212-10](https://asset-proxy-326409019927.us-central1.run.app/?channel=meta&id=1099689749049905&type=video) 42%. The format is a reply to a named customer's comment ("Okay Sarah, love your picks") followed by her order being packed on camera. The viewer watches their own order happen before they place it. This is the comment-reply hook the research corpus flagged as theory-only; it is now confirmed in-account, and it is the cheapest trust device we have (no creator fee, no script).

## 3. What this means for LPs, site, email and SMS

Ten transfers from paid creative to owned channels, each backed by a number above or a comment pattern below.

- **Show the box, not the bag.** The shipper box with 12 bags spilling out is the highest-converting image in the account (39 to 46% ATC on $50k+). LP hero, PDP first image, email header, SMS MMS: the open box with every flavor visible. Not one bag on a plinth.

- **Lead with 70 calories against a number they already know.** "This one bag is 880 calories." "Half a cookie has more sugar than this whole bag." Put a same-number comparison row above the fold on every page and in the first screen of every email: 70 cal vs 70 cal of the candy you buy now, then 3g vs 23g sugar, 26g vs no fiber. The best page (TRW-Gifts) already opens on a comparison table; the others should.

- **State the price before they scroll.** $2.69 a bag, 46% off + free gifts, in the ad, at the top of the page, in the subject line. Ads that anchor price close at 17.9% ATC to order versus 9 to 12% for ads that don't. Email and SMS should say the per-bag price, not "fiber-packed gummies".

- **Kill curiosity and negation copy on owned channels.** "We can't say..." opens a loop a click resolves; on a page or an email there is no click, only delay. The 12 to 16% ATC these ads earn is what happens when a visitor arrives without the basics.

- **Answer the five questions people ask every week, on-page and pre-click.** Ingredients and sweetener (name erythritol and sucralose, say why), total vs net carbs (37g total, 3g net, show the subtraction), pieces per bag (16 to 18), which flavors are not sour (name three), one-time purchase available. Net-carb confusion is the number-one pre-purchase blocker in both digests. Build a "the label math" block on the LP, a FAQ footer in email, one line in SMS.

- **Subscription clarity above the fold.** The most repeated hostile comment is auto-enroll; an identical three-paragraph complaint is being pasted across three ads. The ad that carried the overlay "No subscription required. One-time orders available at checkout." ran a $65 CPA. Put that sentence on the LP offer block, the cart, the welcome email and a pinned comment on every live ad.

- **Retail proof travels.** "Sold out at Target three times this month" is the best static family. "Are these in stores?" is asked 10+ times a week. Site header, welcome email, PDP: Target, Amazon rank, review count. Countable proof, not adjectives.

- **Menu beats hero.** The best static is a six-bag grid with a badge and a one-line note per bag. The collection page and every email product grid should carry the badge system (Best Seller, Fan Favorite, Cult Classic, Sour Pick) and one line per flavor, not names alone. It pre-orients the visitor so ATC fires fast.

- **Real orders being packed.** The warehouse pack-an-order videos hold the top three ATC rates. Post-purchase email: "your box is being packed" with a 10-second clip; site: an orders-packed-today strip; and reply to real comments with real packing.

- **Voice.** The TRW-Gifts listicle is the tone standard because it is funny and physical ("feels like eating a yoga mat"). The haul creator's "how is this even legal?" is the same register. The GLP-1 page states the same benefits in a clinical voice and converts less at checkout. Every reason carries a bit, the joke targets the category, Shameless is the straight man.

## 4. What the customer cares about

From what converts in creative, cross-checked against roughly 17,000 Facebook ad comments (the two newest weekly digests, Aug 25 to Sep 8) and the Amazon and DTC review corpus.

- **It has to be real candy first.** Taste surprise is the number-one unprompted review theme ("makes no sense they taste as good as they do"). Every winning ad shows candy eaten or piled up; the winning hook cycle is candy, then the catch. Fiber-first opens lose on the same footage.

- **How much am I getting for the money.** Hauls win because the box is the value. The top price objection is per-piece math: "only 15 or 16 candies per bag", "$7 for 5.5 oz". The highest-liked positive comment of the fortnight did the counter-math for us: _"A candy bar is nearly $3 for one and these cost less than that per bag and have fiber and WAY less sugar."_ That is the price frame.

- **Calories and sugar they can check.** They read the label. "37 grams of carbs!" is the loudest confusion, which means the number they see has to be the number we lead with, and the net-carb subtraction has to be visible. The parity device (70 vs 70) is how the product survives that scrutiny.

- **What is actually in it.** Sweetener anxiety (erythritol, sucralose) is roughly 40 comments a week and shifting from taste to safety, with people citing studies. No live creative addresses it. Silence reads as hiding; naming the sweetener and the reason is the only move.

- **Permission, not a diet.** "I allow myself one bag a day", "on a weight loss journey and these are a real treat", the 75-year-old grandmother who "wasted a lot of money" on alternatives. The buyer is dieting and wants candy back. Explicit weight framing loses (0.36 to 0.40 CM-ROAS, and Meta policy); "keep candy in your life" wins.

- **Control over the purchase.** No subscription trap, order once, know where the package is. Trust is a conversion variable: the scam accusations sit on the two biggest videos and go unanswered.

- **Range and discovery.** "Which ones aren't sour", eight independent black-licorice requests in a week, chili mango restock. They want to browse, which is why the menu static and "get all four" beat "pick one".

- **They share the regularity joke, they buy the candy.** "Can't poop? Eat this candy" is the top TikTok Shop earner ($47k GMV) and poop-first statics lose money on Meta (0.42 CM). Bathroom humor is a creator and comment lever, not a paid-static lever.
