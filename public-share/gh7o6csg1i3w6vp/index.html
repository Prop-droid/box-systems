Findings

Three findings

They changed how the account spends. Strategy is not taste plus a moodboard: it is finding the thing in the data everyone's incentives point away from, then turning it into briefs.

01

Finding 01 · Creative analytics

Hook rate lies

The whole industry briefs for hook rate. I bucketed 149 videos and $681k of spend by it: hook rate was inversely correlated with ROAS. Click-through doubled across the buckets while conversion halved. Loud openers buy viewers who never buy; every video above breakeven was a quiet earner.

Ranking axis rebuilt around contribution-margin ROAS and CVR. Hook rate demoted to a diagnostic. We stopped killing quiet earners and stopped scaling loud losers.

03

Finding 03 · Funnel forensics

Cold clicks

A top-spend landing page "decayed" from 50% add-to-cart to 18% in ten weeks. The reflex is fix the page. I correlated 60+ creatives feeding it and found the real variable: cost per LP view set both add-to-cart and checkout close. The page never decayed. The traffic got cheaper.

LPs now ranked by click-price cohort instead of blended conversion, and high-CTR curiosity ads get briefed with the price anchored up front, because their visitors arrive without one.

Doctrine

Hard rules

Earned, not borrowed. Each one cost real spend to learn.

  • Iterate winners. New concepts are bets, sized like bets. 43% of all winners are retros of other winners. Hooks on a proven body is the cheapest slot machine in any account.
  • Statics are the winner factory. Video is where proven angles scale. Cheap statics find the angle at 3 to 4 burned variants per survivor. The video budget goes to what already won.
  • Measure their product, not yours. Comparative claims land harder when the number on screen belongs to the thing the buyer already eats.
  • Entertainment first. Nobody watches an ad that feels like one. The product reveal belongs inside the narrative, not instead of it.
  • Rank on contribution margin. Everything else is a diagnostic. Hook rate, CTR and view counts all have inverse or null correlation with money in accounts I've measured. Margin doesn't lie.
